Capstone: assess Seat-Heat's change management

A paper assessment of one process, SUP.10, with a target of level 2. Everything from lessons 1–8 turns up here, deliberately mixed. Try each question before you look anything up.

The assessment input

PurposeOEM supplier assessment ahead of the start-of-production decision.
Org. unitThe Seat-Heat team, one site.
ProcessSUP.10 Change Request Management, target capability level 2.
ContextAll change requests for releases 2.0 to 2.3. Two instances: application code (Seat-Heat's own) and platform code (a shared base-software layer that Seat-Heat maintains for three projects).

SUP.10 in brief

Purpose: "to ensure that change requests are recorded, analyzed, tracked, approved, and implemented". The base practices are BP1 identify and record · BP2 analyse and assess · BP3 approve before implementation · BP4 bidirectional traceability · BP5 confirm implementation · BP6 track to closure[PAM pp. 85–86]. Guideline rules you will need[Guidelines §3.26, pp. 208–211]:

  • SUP.10.RL.1 If the analysis omits potential side effects, BP2 shall not be rated F.
  • SUP.10.RL.3 If not all relevant disciplines or stakeholders are in the approval authority, BP3 shall not be rated F.
  • SUP.10.RL.4 If approval decisions are apparently not taken, or not taken in time, without justification, BP3 shall be downrated.

The evidence: application-code instance

Jira issue type "Change Request". Mandatory fields: initiator, reason, description, affected components, effort, risk. Workflow: Open → Analysed → Approved → Implemented → Verified → Closed. Issue history intact back to release 2.0. Sample of 10 CRs, chosen by the assessor, traced from OEM request to release: · all 10 recorded with initiator and reason; · 10 of 10 list affected components; 3 of 10 say nothing about side effects on other functions; · CCB (product owner + SW lead) meets weekly; the test lead is not a member; minutes in Confluence, one page per meeting, edited the same week; · 2 of 10 went Open → Implemented, skipping Approved ("urgent customer fix"); no emergency path is defined; · every CR links to the requirement issues it changes and to its PRs; PRs cite the CR key; CRs raised from bugs link to the bug; · the test lead sets Verified only after the linked HIL tests pass; · a dashboard shows CR status; the OEM portal is synced weekly. Project handbook: "Every CR analysed within 5 working days. No unanalysed CR 4 weeks before a release." Dashboard of analysis age reviewed at stand-up. It showed the 5-day target missed in sprints 12 and 13; no action was recorded. The sprint plan reserves the CCB's two hours a week and names the product owner and SW lead for it. CCB membership and the OEM portal channel are defined in the handbook. A Confluence page lists "analysis complete" criteria; the CCB checks them before approving. A tool template enforces the fields.

Round 4: write it like an assessor

Write the weakness statement for BP3 in the application instance: what is missing, the evidence, and the risk.

"Change requests are not consistently approved before implementation: 2 of 10 sampled CRs (the two 'urgent customer fix' CRs) moved from Open to Implemented without CCB approval, and no emergency approval path is defined. The CCB does not include the test lead. Risk: changes reach the product without assessed impact on verification or agreed release scope."

In three sentences, explain to Seat-Heat's manager why SUP.10 got level 1.

Level 2 needs PA 1.1 fully achieved across both instances, and ours aggregates to largely achieved: the application code sometimes skips side-effect analysis and approval, and the platform code has almost no change process. The application team's management setup is good, but it isn't enough while the platform instance pulls every attribute down. Build a real change process for platform code, then close the approval and side-effect gaps, and level 2 is within reach.

Where to go from here

  • Deepen per process. For each process you meet in real life, read its PAM pages and then its chapter in the Guidelines (chapter 3 for level 1). The rating rules show where assessors actually look.
  • Test your judgement against people. An intacs Gate4SPICE event is the lowest-cost way to hear practising assessors reason about ratings.
  • Revisit in a week. Retake the warm-ups of lessons 2–8 without rereading the lessons. What you can't retrieve is what to review, and spacing is where long-term retention comes from.
