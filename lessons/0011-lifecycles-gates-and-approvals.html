Lifecycles, gates and approvals

ASPICE defines no phases, so no gate model can "map to the phases of the V". What a gate model can do is leave exactly the records an assessor looks for. This lesson shows which records.

ASPICE has processes, not phases

A PRM/PAM "neither predefines, nor discourages, any order in which PRM processes or Base Practices are to be performed"[PAM §3.3.4, pp. 26–27]. Order belongs to the lifecycle a project defines, which is its answer to MAN.3.BP2 "Define the project lifecycle". The V is a picture of which work products must be consistent and traceable with which. It is not a sequence of phases. So:

  • A stage-gate model is a lifecycle, a HOW. It can be a strong one, and the assessor maps it onto the indicators like any other.
  • Iteration is allowed. Agile methods "cannot, by definition, contradict" ASPICE[Guidelines §2.5.2, p. 76]. Going back through a gate is not a deviation.
  • Plans must still meet the customer's release planning. Significant gaps there downrate MAN.3.BP4, BP9 and SPL.2.BP1 (AGE.RL.1)[p. 77].

A new process for the course: SPL.2 Product Release

It is flex, not base, but it is the process a final gate most often lands in. Purpose: "to control the release of a product to the intended customer". Its BPs define release content and criteria, define the package, identify each release uniquely, build it "from items under configuration control", ensure approval before delivery, provide a release note, state the support terms, and deliver[PAM pp. 30–32]. The approval record, 13-13 Product release approval, identifies the release date and "name and role of approver"[Annex B, p. 138].

Two things gates are often credited with, wrongly

  • System requirements are analysed in SYS.2, not SYS.1. SYS.1 is Requirements Elicitation: it gathers and analyses stakeholder needs and their changes. Software requirements are SWE.1.
  • Independent verification is not an ASPICE demand. In PAM 4.1 only quality assurance must be independent (SUP.1.BP1, "no self-monitoring")[p. 79]. ASPICE doesn't require independent verification. Other standards your project follows, such as ISO 26262, have their own independence rules: check which apply, and credit the right standard.

Someone says, "Our gates map to the ASPICE V-model phases." Correct them in two sentences.

ASPICE defines no phases or order; the V shows which work products must be consistent and traceable with which. The gates are our lifecycle (the HOW), and each should leave the records an assessor looks for: baselines, approvals with name and role, and traced verification results.

Your win: you can present any stage-gate lifecycle for what it is and point to the evidence each gate should leave behind.
