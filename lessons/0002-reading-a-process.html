Reading a process

Every one of the 32 processes is written to the same template. Learn to read one and you can read them all, and you can judge whether a team reaches level 1 on it.

PA 1.1 L · PA 2.1 L · PA 2.2 F. Which level?

"Every Jira story must have an acceptance-criteria field filled in." WHAT, HOW or DOING?

The anatomy of a process

Chapter 4 describes every process with the same four parts[Table 10, p. 28]:

PartWhat it isHow to use it
PurposeOne sentence: why the process exists.The test of last resort: is this achieved?
OutcomesA numbered list of observable results.This is what PA 1.1 rates.
Base practicesBP1…BPn: activities that achieve the outcomes. Process-specific.Activity-oriented evidence: "do they do this?"
Output information itemsNumbered items such as 08-56 Schedule, described in Annex B.Result-oriented evidence: "what should I find?"

A table at the end of each process says which BP and which information item support which outcome. That table is the assessor's map.

PA 1.1 in one line

Level 1 has a single attribute with a single generic practice: GP 1.1.1 "Achieve the process outcomes", which means achieving the intent of the base practices and producing work products that evidence the outcomes[§5.2.1, p. 100]. So rating PA 1.1 comes down to rating the base practices of that process.

Information items are not documents to write

This is the most common beginner mistake. An information item is what the assessor looks for. A work product is what the team actually produced. There is no 1:1 relationship: one Jira board can contain the characteristics of several information items, and one information item can be spread over many artefacts[§3.3.2, pp. 24–25]. The characteristics in Annex B "shall not be interpreted as a required structure"[p. 24]. Evidence comes from two sources only: work products (including repository content) and testimony from the people doing the work[p. 24].

A ticket in Jira is a work product. The Guidelines say so explicitly: change requests or problem reports held in a workflow tool count[Guidelines §4.2.1, p. 246]. Nobody needs a Word document called "Change Request".

What the assessor found at Seat-Heat

Confluence "Project charter": goals, customer, the ECU variants in and out of scope. Jira: epics per customer release; stories estimated in points for the current sprint only. No estimate of the effort remaining for the features due in the next two releases. Sprint reviews every two weeks; burndown charts; a status mail to the OEM each month. The HVAC team supplies a CAN signal Seat-Heat needs; the dependency lives in one engineer's head. Release plan: three customer releases with dates, consistent with the epics.

The Guidelines contain rating rules written for exactly this situation. One is [AGE.RL.2]: "If remaining effort for features, which are to be delivered in the current or next release, is not estimated, then MAN.3.BP5 shall be downrated"[Guidelines §2.5.2.1, p. 77]. Lesson 8 covers rating rules in full.

C. From memory

Name the four parts of a process description, and say which one PA 1.1 rates.

Purpose, outcomes, base practices and output information items. PA 1.1 rates whether the outcomes are achieved, judged through the base practices (activity indicators) and information items (result indicators).

Your win: you can open any process, map evidence to its base practices and give a defensible PA 1.1 rating. Next: which processes an assessment actually looks at, so you know which dozen of the 32 to learn.
