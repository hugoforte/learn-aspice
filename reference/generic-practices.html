Generic practices, levels 1–3

The same practices applied to every process in scope. Each line: the GP, and the question an assessor asks.

PA 1.1 Process performance

1.1.1Are the process outcomes achieved? (Rated through the process's BPs and information items.)

PA 2.1 Performance management

2.1.1Objectives for this process, and a strategy (tools and CI count)?
2.1.2Activities and work packages planned, estimated, scheduled?
2.1.3People, skills, tools and responsibilities determined?
2.1.4…and actually assigned, qualified and available?
2.1.5Deviations noticed and acted on?
2.1.6Involved parties, responsibilities and communication managed?

Keystone: GP 2.1.1. Should agree with MAN.3.BP4–BP8 within one NPLF step.

PA 2.2 Work product management

2.2.1Content, structure, quality and review criteria defined?
2.2.2Storage, identification, distribution and status model defined?
2.2.3Change control, versioning, baselines actually applied?
2.2.4Reviewed against the criteria, and findings resolved?

Pairs: 2.2.1 + 2.2.4 (quality), 2.2.2 + 2.2.3 (control). Moves with SUP.1.BP2/BP3 and SUP.8.BP2/BP4/BP5.

PA 3.1 Process definition

3.1.1Standard process with activities, inputs/outputs, sequence, roles, templates, unambiguous tailoring guidelines, maintained from feedback?
3.1.2Competencies per role, and ways to acquire them?
3.1.3Resources and infrastructure determined?
3.1.4Methods to monitor whether the standard works?

PA 3.2 Process deployment

3.2.1Defined process tailored from the standard, conformance verified, used?
3.2.2People in roles by competency; gaps closed?
3.2.3Resources actually available and used?
3.2.4Performance data collected, analysed and fed back?

Level 3 ignores CM and storage of the process descriptions themselves.

Level 1 vs 2 vs 3, in one line each

1: the outcomes happen. 2: they happen on purpose, against targets, with controlled work products. 3: they happen through an organisational standard, tailored by rule and improved from feedback.
