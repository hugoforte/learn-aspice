# Mission: Automotive SPICE 4.1, the vital 20 %

## Why

Become well versed in Automotive SPICE 4.1 in as little time as possible: learn the few ideas that decide most ratings well enough to read the model fluently, judge a real project's processes the way an assessor would, and hold your own in a conversation with an assessor or an OEM's supplier-quality engineer.

## Success looks like

- Explain the grid (processes × capability levels) and derive a capability level from PA ratings without looking anything up.
- Read any process in the PAM (purpose, outcomes, base practices, information items) and say what evidence would show it is performed.
- Name the recommended VDA Scope and say what each of its processes is for.
- Tell a level 1 weakness from a level 2 weakness from a level 3 weakness, and say which generic practice it hits.
- Spot the consistency and traceability gaps an assessor would find in a modern Git, pull request and CI workflow.
- Run a paper assessment of a small project: sample evidence, rate PAs, aggregate instances and write a weakness statement that would survive review.

## Constraints

- First contact with ASPICE and its relatives (CMMI, ISO/IEC 330xx): start from zero.
- 80/20: every lesson teaches only what moves ratings; the whole course is short enough to finish in a few sittings.
- Primary sources are the VDA PAM 4.1 preview and the VDA Guidelines (3rd ed., for PAM 4.1). Neither may be redistributed, so lessons link to them by page rather than copying them.

## Out of scope

- Becoming a certified (intacs) assessor.
- Hardware (HWE) and machine learning (MLE, SUP.11) engineering in detail.
- Capability levels 4 and 5 beyond knowing what they mean.
- ASPICE for Cybersecurity and the functional safety standards (ISO 26262, ISO/SAE 21434).
