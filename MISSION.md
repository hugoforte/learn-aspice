# Mission: Automotive SPICE, read through rig

## Why

Use the Automotive SPICE 4.1 capability model as a mirror on rig's own way of working: rate the processes rig carries (planning, designing, building, reviewing, landing a work) the way an assessor would, and see what level 2 or 3 would actually demand.

## Success looks like

- Name the ASPICE processes that rig's workflow actually performs (for example MAN.3, SUP.8, SUP.10, PIM.3) and the ones it does not touch.
- Rate one of them against PA 1.1, PA 2.1 and PA 2.2 on the N/P/L/F scale, citing real rig records (work records, gates, PRs, context docs) as objective evidence.
- Derive the resulting capability level from those ratings without looking up the rule.
- Write a one-page capability profile for rig, with the concrete gap to the next level for each process rated.

## Constraints

- First contact with ASPICE and its relatives (CMMI, ISO/IEC 330xx): start from zero.
- Primary source is the VDA PAM 4.1 preview (2026-04-16); it may not be redistributed, so lessons link to it rather than copy it.
- Short lessons; one tangible win each.

## Out of scope

- Becoming a certified (intacs) assessor.
- The engineering process groups that assume hardware or ML (HWE, MLE, SUP.11) and the detail of SYS/SWE, except where rig's records touch them.
- Capability levels 4 and 5 beyond knowing what they mean.
